03 — Why Now
Why Now

Four things changed in the last three years.

Ingredient cost is the driver

Grocery inflation has turned home cooking from a lifestyle choice into a financial one. Households are cooking more, and cooking bigger, than they can finish.

Cooking up · portions up

Loneliness is policy now

Both the US Surgeon General and the UK government named social connection a public-health priority. Councils and health systems are actively buying connection programs.

Budgets are opening

The behaviour is normal

Too Good To Go has 100M+ users. Olio, Buy Nothing and Nextdoor proved that neighbours will transact with strangers on their own street, at scale.

Zero education required

Kitchens are the cheapest supply chain

No rent, no staff, no equipment capex, no dark kitchens. Every home kitchen is idle capacity that costs nothing to onboard and nothing to keep.

Marginal cost ≈ 0

07 — Competition
Competition

Nobody owns the meal next door.

Potluck Delivery marketplaces Food-waste apps Neighbourhood social
Home-cooked supply Core — — —
Cook earns from it ● At cost, always Restaurants only Surplus, discounted —

Recurring, scheduled meals ● Nightly ● — —
Money stays in the street ● 100% — Partial N/A

At-cost is a promise they can't copy

An incumbent matching us would have to kill its own take rate. We give that margin to the cook — and it becomes our cheapest acquisition channel.

A trust graph, not a supply list

Address verification, kitchen hygiene certification, mutual neighbour reviews. Reviews between people who see each other weekly behave differently to anonymous ratings.

Supply that can't be aggregated

Cooks are local and unbundled. No platform can nationalise a street, and no competitor can onboard our cooks faster than we can.
