The problem 02 / 08

Every notes app made
the same silent bet.

That the network would always be there. In tunnels, on flights, in basements, factories and mountain huts, it isn’t — and your notes paid the price.

01 — DEAD ZONES

No signal, no notes

Tessel 1 could refuse to open a notebook that was already sitting on your own disk. A spinner is not an interface.

02 — ROUND TRIPS

Every keystroke, a request

A save only counted once it had visited our servers. Our uptime quietly became your typing speed.

03 — SYNC ANXIETY

“Did it save?”

Offline-first 03 / 08

Notes that write
to disk first.

Tessel 2 inverts the order of operations. The local file is the source of truth; the network is an optimisation that happens when it happens.

  • Full editing, notebooks, tags and search with the radio switched off.
  • Instant local index — results in under 30 ms across 100,000 notes.
  • Conflicts merge deterministically. Nothing is ever silently overwritten.
  • No “connecting…” state. There is nothing to connect to.
Always on

Local vault

Every note is a file on your disk. Read, write, search, rename — no round trip.

Always on

Merge engine

Edits land in an append-only log with deterministic CRDT semantics.

If available

Transport

Peer-to-peer over LAN or the open internet. Relay only when P2P can’t connect.

Off the path

Tessel infrastructure

Not required to read, write or sync. Downtime is now a non-event.

Peer-to-peer sync 04 / 08

Sync without
the middleman.

Your devices talk to each other directly — across the room or across the world.

Tessel 1 your device → our servers → your other device
Tessel 2 your device ↔ your other device
  • Devices discover each other on the local network and connect directly over WebRTC.
  • CRDT merge means every device converges on the same document, order-independent.
  • A relay forwards encrypted packets when P2P fails. It cannot read them.
  • Sync keeps working if our servers are down. Which they were, once. Sorry.
Privacy & security 05 / 08

Encrypted before it
ever leaves the device.

Peer-to-peer removes the server from the middle. Encryption makes sure that even when a relay is used, there is nothing there to read.

  • Per-vault keys, generated on device, sealed with XChaCha20-Poly1305.
  • Keys never leave your devices. We cannot read your notes and cannot reset your keys.
  • Pairing by QR code with a short authentication string you verify by eye.
  • Open-source crypto core; independent third-party audit completed February 2025.
