The problem

Process heat is the last
un‑electrified load.

Industry has decarbonised its electricity and is starting on transport. The heat inside the plant — the steam and hot water that actually makes the product — has barely moved.

20%
of global CO₂ emissions come from industrial process heat. It is the single hardest sector to abate and the least funded.
74%
of that heat is still made on site, by burning gas, oil or coal in a boiler that was installed to last forty years.
<5%
of stack heat is recovered today. The rest leaves the chimney at 200–350 °C — already paid for, already on site.
02
The waste

The heat is already paid for.

A 2 MW gas boiler burns roughly €640k of fuel a year. Eighteen percent of it goes straight up the stack.

That flue gas is too hot to dump and too cool to feed back into the boiler. So it is vented — for the entire thirty‑year life of the asset.

Waste heat is free fuel. No new gas contract, no new grid connection, no permitting.
Every site we target already has the flue, the pipework and the controls we need to tap into.
At our beachhead sites, that stack loss is worth 2.1 GWh a year per plant.

03
The solution

A heat‑pump module that bolts onto
the boiler you already own.

01

Bolt on

Skid‑mounted module taps the flue downstream of the boiler. Three‑day install, no production shutdown, no boiler modification, no re‑permitting.

02

Lift the heat

A high‑temperature heat pump upgrades 90 °C flue gas to 180 °C process heat — delivering 3.4 kWh of heat for every 1 kWh of electricity.

03

Return it

Hot water or low‑pressure steam feeds the plant's existing header. The boiler keeps running — it simply burns less gas for the same output.

Retrofit, not rebuild.  The capital is already sunk in the boiler. We monetise the part of it that is being thrown away.
04
How it works

Four stages, one skid, no downtime.

01 — Source

Boiler flue

320 °C

Flue gas leaves the boiler through a stack the plant already owns. Today it goes straight to atmosphere.

02 — Recover

Recovery exchanger

90 °C out

A fin‑and‑tube exchanger draws 62% of the heat out of the flue. Materials are selected around the acid dew point, not around guesswork.

03 — Upgrade

Heat pump loop

COP 3.4

A closed low‑GWP refrigerant loop lifts the recovered heat to 180 °C using a third of the energy it delivers.

04 — Deliver

Process header

180 °C

Hot water or 3‑bar steam feeds the existing plant header. Downstream equipment sees no change at all.

06
Why now

Three curves crossed in the
last eighteen months.

This was technically possible a decade ago and commercially impossible. That has flipped.

01

High‑temp heat pumps became off‑the‑shelf

Compressors and low‑GWP refrigerants rated to 200 °C are now catalogue parts from three suppliers. We are integrating proven hardware, not inventing a compressor.

02

Cheap power meets flat industrial load

Industrial demand is constant, so it sits on the best hours of the grid. Off‑peak and negative‑price windows are multiplying — and thermal storage lets a plant bank that energy as heat.

03

Carbon got a price and a signature

Rising EU ETS costs and CSRD Scope 1 disclosure put the plant manager's own name on the emissions line. Efficiency stopped being a facilities project and became a board item.
